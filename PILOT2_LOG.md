# Pilot 2 log and deviations

Every change below is logged before the runs it affects.

## 20 Nov

- **Tests.** All ten sanity tests in `pilot2/test_world2.py` pass. Three test set-ups were corrected before passing, none touching an outcome measure: the wear test needed more steps (8,000) and no home pull to show wear clearly; the filtered-sampler test froze angles so that "home" and "current" coincide.

- **Q-tuning, first pass (seed 2000).** With ε = 0.05 and the usual initialisation, competence stays at 0.66–0.77 after 15,000 steps for learning rates 0.02–0.1, and never reaches 0.9. Building is also weaker than in pilot 1: under wear the built share is 0.05 / 0.16 / 0.63 at η = 0.005 / 0.02 / 0.08; under shaping by eye at κ = 1, 0.09 / 0.20 / 0.56 at s_u = 0.01 / 0.03 / 0.1; at κ = 0.25 at most 0.12.
- **D3, exploration under the skip rule.** An agent whose predictions start near 0.5 sits below the 2/3 act threshold, skips almost every turn, and learns only from ε-acts; refusals then push its predictions further down. Builders act rarely for the same reason, which weakens building. Tuning allowed by the spec (competence only): ε in {0.05, 0.2} and an optimistic output bias at initialisation (b2 = 0 or +1.5, so starting predictions are about 0.82 and the agent acts until refused). Proposed for PLAN.md whichever passes; noted as a design consequence of the skip rule.

- **D3 result (seed 2000).** No setting reaches competence 0.9 within 15,000 steps (best 0.85 at one check, most 0.66–0.80). Over 40,000 steps, ε = 0.2 with learning rate 0.05 climbs to 0.84–0.86; ε = 0.1 and learning rate 0.02 are similar or slower. The optimistic bias didn't help. Fixed for pilot 2: ε = 0.2, learning rate 0.05, no bias.
- **D4, budgets and the competence criterion (pilot 2 only).** Learning under the skip rule is about three times slower than in pilot 1. For pilot 2, learning cost and check Q2 use competence 0.75 instead of 0.9; raising budgets are 10,000 and 30,000 steps (instead of 5,000 and 15,000); E8's pretraining cap is 40,000 steps and T2 is 20,000. Proposed for PLAN.md: either keep 0.9 with longer runs, or make the angle easier to read (smaller appearance weights in the body map), chosen on competence alone.
- **Q4 re-sweep, with ε = 0.2.** Adds η = 0.15 for wear and s_u = 0.3 for shaping by eye at κ = 0.25, since κ = 0.25 reached at most 0.12 before.
- **Q4 re-sweep result (seed 2000).** Wear: built share 0.13 / 0.63 / 0.89 at η = 0.02 / 0.08 / 0.15. Shaping by eye at κ = 0.25: 0.06 / 0.12 / 0.47 at s_u = 0.03 / 0.1 / 0.3; at κ = 1: 0.19 / 0.58 / 0.93. Q4 passes for all three rules. For the readouts, pilot 2 uses the strongest setting of each: wear η = 0.15, shaping by eye s_u = 0.3 at both κ. At κ = 0.25 the built world still has 34% edge items against 44% natural, so E8–E10 under that rule can only show small effects.

- **First run done (seeds 2000–2002, about 160 s per seed).** Results in `pilot2/results/`, figures from `pilot2/verify2.py`.
- **D5, the edges are too hard for any agent to read.** Wild-raised agents' edge accuracy is 0.64–0.68 (chance is 0.5), and on decisive edge turns width-32 agents get −0.11 to 0.18 of the optimal payoff: they are rarely confident enough to act, so they skip. The same was true in pilot 1 (0.71). With nobody knowing the edges well, built-raised agents can't be much worse, and E8 has little to forget. One design check, on wild agents only and seed 2000 only: scale the appearance inputs of every body by λ in {1, 0.5, 0.25} (making the angle easier to see) and record wild width-32 agents' competence and edge accuracy after 30,000 steps. No built-raised agent and no prediction is involved.

Not adopted in pilot 2's readouts (they all use λ = 1); proposed for version 4.
