## 2. What the manifestation results say

- **Building emerges (E1)**, as drainage out of the overlaps rather than an arbitrary convention (section 2a). Its amount follows an upkeep law (E2).
  - These slopes come from shaping by eye at κ = 1 in the symmetric world.
  - The built-item count tracks M·s_u/δ. Cells with the same M·s_u/δ hold the same count whatever the mix: 47–49 items at 600 (five cells, M from 1 to 16) and 58–60 at 800 (three cells) (`results/e2/analysis_output.txt`, cell table).
  - The count rises slightly sublinearly with M·s_u/δ (slopes 0.91 to 0.98), as a saturating pool would.
  - **Why the slopes collapse without the strength covariate.** Cells entered the fit only if their built share lay inside the 0.1–0.9 band (D2). To stay inside it, a cell with more builders or slower decay needed weaker shaping. Among the chosen cells, log M and log(1/δ) each correlate −0.44 with log s_u (`results/e2/selection_note.txt`). Leaving s_u out of the fit then lets its opposing effect cancel most of M's and δ's: an omitted-variable bias that the selection created, not evidence against the law.
  - **Width:** extra builder width above the competence threshold adds nothing. Widths 16, 32 and 64 at one cell give the same count (slope 0.001). This says nothing about networks too small to learn the task.
- **The builders' key: shared versus key-specific gains (E3, shaping by eye at κ = 0.25).**
  - **Compression at the gate is almost all key-specific (L3c):** the entropy drop is 0.63 bits for the builders' own map, 0.52 for other maps of the same type, and 0.045 for outsiders. So 0.59 bits (93%) of the builders' drop isn't shared.
  - **Code length is mostly shared (L3d):** outsiders save 416 bits and the builders' body 531. The key-specific part is 115 bits (22%), so the ratio is 1.27 and fails the 1.5 bar.
  - The learning measures fall between: early learning-curve gain 1.56 × outsiders' (pass, narrowly), 2k-step log loss 1.73 × (pass).
  - **Reading:** building at κ = 0.25 also moves the angle a little (built share 0.60 in these worlds), which every reader can use, while the appearance it shapes compresses only for readers of that axis. Entropy separates by the axis read (same type ≈ same body), as pilot 3 suggested; learning speed favours the exact map, modestly.
  - Under wear, every reader gains alike.
- **Routes leave traces in the world (E4–E6), but the behavioural test can't speak to conventions (section 2a).**
  - Newborns raised in the same built world agree almost perfectly on overlap items (κ 0.99–1.00) and share representations (E_K 0.83); newborns from separately built worlds agree less than natural-world pairs (κ 0.80–0.84) and, under shaping by eye, barely share representational neighbourhoods (E_K 0.07).
  - L4 fails because natural-world pairs already agree at κ = 0.88. That baseline mostly reflects the readout, which picks the nearer arc centre (E6: κ 0.83 across bodies). L4's failure is therefore uninformative about whether conventions are arbitrary.
  - The representational measure (L5) separates routes where behaviour can't.
  - After 5/δ steps of abandonment the same-world advantage is gone (L6), and the built share decays at the drift rate (sanity check c).
- **No forgetting; the price falls on newcomers (E8, E9, E10; section 2b).**
  - Agents that already know the edges keep that knowledge in a built world: the D3 arm's extra fall is 0.012 and 0.019, against L8a. The preregistered E8 passes L8a by the letter only because its agents were still learning: the built world stopped their edge learning.
  - The price shows when the structure fails (E9). After a snap back to home values, built-raised agents earn 42% (wear) and 73% (shaping by eye) less on decisive turns than wild-raised agents over the first 500 steps.
  - The surprise jump at the snap is smaller than predicted (L9b fails). Whether it is concentrated on edge items is untested: only whole-world log loss was recorded.
  - The population keeps the built share at 0.95–0.99 while individuals in the primary E8 sit at edge accuracy 0.69–0.76 (`results/e8/summary_E8_traj.csv`): upkeep is collective, competence individual.

### 2a. What this design can and can't say about arbitrary conventions

Since version 3, neither agents nor builders ever choose between actions. The world offers one action per turn and the agent acts or skips. Two consequences follow, both found in review after the runs:
- **E1/E2 building is real but not conventional.** An item in an overlap zone is pulled toward whichever action happened to be offered when it was used. Once it crosses out of the overlap only one action accepts it, so items drain out of the overlaps. Where an item ends up reflects the offer sequence and the geometry, not a habit a population settled on. The built share and the upkeep law measure that drainage.
- **E4/E6's high natural-world agreement mostly reflects the readout.** Each agent's "chosen action" is read as the action it rates most likely to be accepted. A learner is more confident deeper inside an arc, so in an overlap this picks the nearer arc centre. Two naturally raised newborns therefore agree (κ 0.88, and 0.83 across body types) before anything is built.
- **So L4's failure says nothing about whether conventions are arbitrary.** The design can't produce or detect one. Testing that needs agents that choose freely among actions, as in version 1, in the symmetric world; that would be a new brief.
- **The builders'-key result (E3) is unaffected.** It comes from shaping by eye moving appearance, which doesn't depend on action choice. L5 still shows that worlds built separately leave newborns with different representations.

### 2b. A built world doesn't erase what was learned; it stops newcomers acquiring it

Here a **newcomer** is any agent that learns the domain inside the built world, newborn or not. Three cases, by how much an agent knew of the edges on entering:
- **Fully trained (D3 arm, 60k pretraining, edge accuracy 0.87–0.89 on entry):** nothing lost. Over 30k steps in a built world, the extra fall is 0.012 (wear) and 0.019 (shaping by eye), against L8a.
- **Partly trained (preregistered E8; pretraining stopped at edge accuracy 0.70–0.80):** these agents kept what they had, but in the built world gained only +0.04 to +0.06, against +0.15 for the same agents in the natural control.
- **Untrained (E9, E10; newborns raised in the built world):** they never learned the edges. In E10 their confident error is 1.8–4.0 × wild agents', with boundaries displaced 3.7–5.2° under the skip rule; in E9 their decisive payoff is 42–73% below wild after the snap.

**The claim these data support:** a built world doesn't erase what was learned before entering it; it stops anyone learning there from acquiring what the built world no longer shows. The lifecycle's "stops tracking" step is therefore non-acquisition by newcomers, not forgetting by those who already knew.

## 3. Naive realism (secondary)

- **Silence and confidence.** Built-raised agents are less surprised at home than wild agents, yet confidently wrong on unbuilt edges 1.8–4.0 times as often, with 1.8–2.6 times the edge log loss. This holds under both feedback conditions, against equal-update wild agents, and for all three ensemble kinds, including randomized priors. The matched-surprise test (L10a-matched) couldn't be run: home log-loss distributions never overlapped enough for three bins, so it gives no verdict. The 2-bin numbers are listed in the table as description only.
- **Where boundaries go.** Under the skip rule, built-raised arcs stretch 3.7–5.2° further than wild agents', and the stretch scales with how much building erased (L10-dose). Restoring far-side refusals through the learner's own random acts removes it (L10m: 4.0 → 0.2° under wear, 5.4 → 0.9° under shaping by eye). In this sweep **only the learner's ε_min changes**; the builders keep the same fixed policy throughout, so the built worlds don't change with the condition, as they did in pilot 6. The raw stretch follows acceptances per far-side refusal in rank order (exploratory). This is pilot 6's refusal-starvation account, confirmed on preregistered seeds without its builder confound. Under passive feedback the displacement is small (0.9–1.4°), while the confident error remains.
- **What is and isn't ordinary.** The built-raised agents' error is ordinary extrapolation by networks on inputs their training didn't cover (the edges building emptied). What the setup adds is that the agents' own world removed those inputs, and that their home surprise gives no warning. Section 3a places this against the literature. It isn't forgetting (E8, D3), and the update-count control rules out mere fewer updates.

### 3a. Prior work

Abstracts read on arxiv.org on 21 Nov 2026. Constant et al. (2018) isn't on arXiv, so it wasn't read from the allowed sources; the framing below relies only on its title's claim as the brief states it.
- **Arbitrary conventions in coordination.**
  - Hu, Lerer, Peysakhovich & Foerster (2020, arXiv:2003.02979) show that self-play can produce highly specialised conventions that fail with new partners, and propose other-play, which uses known symmetries of the game to avoid them.
  - Treutlein, Dennis, Oesterheld & Foerster (2021, arXiv:2106.06613) formalise label-free coordination, and argue that arbitrary tie-breaking between incompatible optima is exactly what zero-shot coordination should prevent.
  - Our setup can't speak to this line of work (section 2a): agents never choose between equally good actions, so no tie is ever broken.
  - The closest analogue is E4/L5: worlds built separately leave newborns with different representations (E_K 0.07 across worlds under shaping by eye, against 0.83 within a world), though their behaviour still agrees, because geometry fixes it.
- **Cultural accumulation.**
  - Cook, Lu, Hughes, Leibo & Foerster (2024, arXiv:2406.00392) obtain accumulation across generations of RL agents by balancing social and independent learning; their generations pass knowledge on through in-context or in-weights learning.
  - Here, what passes from builders to later agents is the state of the world, not anything learned socially.
  - Section 2b's result is in a sense the converse of theirs: what builders put into the world lets newcomers skip learning, and what they don't learn is then missing when the world changes (E9).
- **Stigmergy.**
  - Xu, Li, Zhao & Zhang (2019, arXiv:1911.12504) use a digital pheromone in a shared environment as an indirect channel between independent learners.
  - Our agents also meet only through the items, but nothing is designed as a signal: the "message" is the reshaped item itself. E3 shows its readability depends on the reader's body (entropy drop 0.63 bits for the builders' body, 0.045 for outsiders).
- **Forgetting and plasticity.**
  - Parisi et al.'s review (2018/2019, arXiv:1802.07569) frames catastrophic forgetting as interference under non-stationary data.
  - Dohare et al. (2023, arXiv:2306.13812) show that continually trained deep networks also lose the ability to learn new things.
  - E8 matches neither: the built world withheld data rather than presenting conflicting data, and trained agents didn't lose edge accuracy (D3). We didn't test plasticity directly (the second-task test was dropped in version 6).
  - The deficit we do find is non-acquisition by newcomers (section 2b), which neither framework addresses.
- **Overconfidence away from the data.**
  - Hein, Andriushchenko & Bitterwolf (2019, arXiv:1812.05720) show that piecewise-linear (ReLU) networks are almost always highly confident far from their training data.
  - Our networks use tanh, so their argument doesn't apply directly, but the phenomenon is the same: confident predictions where the training distribution has no support.
  - The new element is that the agents' own world (built by their predecessors) removed the support, and that in-distribution surprise (home log loss) gives no warning.
- **Randomized priors.**
  - Osband, Aslanides & Cassirer (2018, arXiv:1806.03335) add a fixed, untrained random prior network to each ensemble member, so that uncertainty not grounded in data can be expressed.
  - At β = 10 our randomized-prior ensembles passed the manipulation check (member spread at edges 3.7–6.2 × that at centres), yet still met L10a. The pre-stated reading is that the effect isn't specific to single networks: prior-driven disagreement wasn't enough to stop confident error at edges that building emptied.
- **Niche construction.** Constant, Ramstead, Veissière, Campbell & Friston (2018), as BRIEF §1 notes: the "niche as the environment's estimate of the organism" reading predicts E3's same-body advantage too, so E3 doesn't distinguish it from the theory under test. Its abstract wasn't read here (not on arXiv).

### 5a. Post hoc analyses after review (24 Nov; not preregistered)
None changes a preregistered verdict. `python runs/run.py e10_outsider 0-9`, then `python analysis/e10_extra.py`.
- **L10m extras** (`results/e10/summary_L10m_extra.csv`, same agents as L10m): as ε_min rises 0.02 → 0.1 → 0.3, the confident-error ratio (built ÷ wild) falls 2.70 → 1.65 → 1.49 (wear) and 4.02 → 2.83 → 2.52 (shaping by eye); payoff per decisive edge turn at the moment of snap-back is 51% → 32% → 26% (wear) and 90% → 66% → 53% (shaping by eye) below wild. That payoff is the zero-step point of the E9 measure; the 500-step E9 run exists only at ε_min 0.02 (42%, 73%). The stretch nearly vanishes, but the confident error doesn't.
- **D5, outsider-body newcomers** (`results/e10_outsider/summary_D5.csv`): confident-error ratio built ÷ wild, same body vs outsider body, shaping by eye: 4.02 vs 3.22 (skip), ratio of ratios 1.25 [1.09, 1.44]; 2.65 vs 2.11 (passive), 1.26 [1.05, 1.51]. Wear control 0.99 [0.84, 1.16] and 0.86 [0.64, 1.14]. The appearance-shortcut reading stated in D5 is supported; most of the effect remains for outsiders.
- **Absolute confident-error rates** (`results/e10/summary_excess_share.csv`, `summary_dose_rates.csv`; final review): newcomers' rate minus wild agents' rate on edge test items is 9.1 points (wear) and 15.9 (shaping by eye) under the skip rule. The share of that excess left once refusals are restored is 53% and 58% under passive feedback and 37% and 57% at ε_min 0.3; for outsider-body newcomers, who can't use the appearance shortcut, 66% and 50% of their own excess under passive feedback. So "much", not "most", of the error comes from the removed edge items.
